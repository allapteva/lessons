Verb patterns

After some verbs we use to, after others -ing, and after a few just the verb itself. Start with the reference cards, then practise, moving from controlled exercises to your own sentences.

to + infinitive

We decided to go.

-ing form

I felt like going.

bare infinitive

They let us go.

    1

    The patterns at a glance

    Come back to these cards whenever you are unsure. Tap the speaker to hear an example.

    A · verb + to + infinitive

    want, decide, hope, plan, offer, promise, refuse, manage, seem, pretend, learn, afford, forget, need, try

    • We managed to get tickets for the matinee.
    • I can't afford to sit in the stalls this time.
    • She decided not to stay for the second half.

    Negative: not goes before to. Also: after adjectives (It's easy to get lost) and to give a reason (I went to the box office to buy tickets). Careful: forget and try can also take -ing, with a different meaning (see card G).

    B · verb + -ing

    enjoy, mind, finish, feel like, spend time, avoid, keep, stop, imagine, can't stand, suggest, practise, miss

    • I don't mind sitting in the circle.
    • I never feel like going out on New Year's Eve.
    • At weekends I spend hours reading.

    Also: the -ing form can be the subject of a sentence. Dancing is the best way I know to switch off. Careful: stop can also take to, with a different meaning (see card G).

    C · verb + person + to

    want, ask, tell, expect, allow, would like

    • My parents wanted me to spend my evenings studying.
    • I'd like you to come with me.
    • They don't allow people to take photos.

    Not: I want that you come. Negative: She told me not to worry.

    D · bare infinitive (no to)

    make + person, let + person, modal verbs (can, must, should, might…), would rather, had better

    • My parents didn't use to let me stay out late.
    • Our teacher made us read the scene again.
    • I'd rather stay in tonight.
    • We'd better leave now.

    Negatives: I'd rather not go. You'd better not be late.

    E · like, love, hate, prefer
    • I love watching films in 3D. = I love to watch films in 3D.
    • I prefer to stay in. = I prefer staying in.
    • I'd love to see them live. (would + to only)
    • I prefer reading to watching TV.

    Either form, same meaning: like, love, hate, prefer, start, begin. Only to: would like, would love, would hate, would prefer.

    F · preposition + -ing
    • She's very good at dancing.
    • Are you interested in joining our book club?
    • Turn your phone off before going in.
    • I'm looking forward to seeing you.

    Watch out: in look forward to, to is a preposition, so it takes -ing.

    G · same verb, different meaning

    remember, forget, try, stop, regret

    + to+ -ing
    remember + toRemember to book the tickets.Don't forget. The action comes after remembering. + -ingI remember booking them.I have a memory of it. The action came first.
    forget + toI forgot to call her.I didn't do it. + -ingI'll never forget seeing them live.A memory I will always have. Usually with never.
    try + toI tried to stay awake.I made an effort to do something difficult. + -ingTry turning it off and on again.Do it as an experiment, to see if it helps.
    stop + toWe stopped to get a coffee.We paused in order to do it. + -ingWe stopped going to that club.We used to do it, but then we didn't any more.
    regret + toWe regret to inform you…Formal: I'm sorry to give you bad news. + -ingI regret not going.I'm sorry about something in the past.
