        A short history of Thanksgiving

        On the fourth Thursday of November, more than forty million turkeys are eaten in the United States. But the holiday began with a much smaller meal.

        In the autumn of 1621, the Pilgrims, English settlers who had arrived on the Mayflower a year earlier, celebrated their first harvest in Plymouth, in what is now Massachusetts. About ninety men from the Wampanoag people joined them, and the feast lasted three days. Historians think wild birds and deer were eaten, but there was probably no pumpkin pie: the settlers had no ovens.

        For more than two hundred years after that, days of thanksgiving were held now and then, on different dates in different places. Then a magazine editor, Sarah Josepha Hale, started writing letters to presidents, asking for one national holiday. In 1863, in the middle of the Civil War, Abraham Lincoln finally agreed, and Thanksgiving was made a national holiday.

        Today it is a day for family. The turkey is carved at the table, people say what they are thankful for, and the leftovers are eaten for days. Millions of people watch the parade in New York, which has been held every year since 1924, and American football on TV. And every November one lucky turkey is ‘pardoned’ by the President and sent to live on a farm.

              B. Telling someone what was said

              present
              ‘I’m tired.’ → She said (that) she was tired.
              past, present perfect
              ‘I’ve lost my keys.’ → He said he had lost his keys.
              will, can, must
              ‘I’ll call you.’ → She said she would call me. (can → could, must → had to)
              yes / no question
              ‘Are you OK?’ → He asked me if I was OK.
              wh- question
              ‘Where do you live?’ → She asked me where I lived.
              instruction
              ‘Sit down.’ → He told me to sit down. ‘Don’t move.’ → He told me not to move.
              request
              ‘Can you help me?’ → She asked me to help her.
              say or tell?
              She said (that)… · She told me (that)… Tell always needs a person.
              words that change
              today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · yesterday → the day before · here → there · next week → the following week

              In questions there is no do / does / did, and the word order is the same as in a normal sentence: She asked me where I lived, not where did I live.
