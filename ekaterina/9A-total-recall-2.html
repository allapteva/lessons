      What did they say?

      When you tell someone what another person said, the verb usually goes one step back into the past. Remember the four rules, then choose the right form in seven sentences.

      0 / 0

      Statements

      • “I’m tired.” → She said she was tired.
      • “I’ve lost my keys.” → He said he had lost his keys.
      • “I’ll call you.” → She said she would call me.
      • say something, but tell somebody: She told me she was tired.

      Questions and requests

      • No do / did, normal word order: “Where do you live?” → He asked me where I lived.
      • Yes / no questions take if: “Are you OK?” → She asked if I was OK.
      • Instructions: tell / ask + (not) to: “Don’t worry.” → He told me not to worry.

                Films and series

                Why TV patients always survive

                Many doctors love medical dramas, and many doctors can’t watch them. Series like ER, House and Grey’s Anatomy have been watched by millions of people, and for a lot of viewers they are the only picture of a hospital they have.

                The problem is that TV medicine is often too good to be true. In 1996, three American doctors watched almost a hundred episodes of popular series and counted every time a patient’s heart stopped and someone started CPR. On TV, about three out of four of these patients survived. In real life, far fewer do. The researchers said that the series were giving people false hopes, and that this could make difficult decisions even harder for families.

                Other mistakes are smaller. On screen, doctors often use a defibrillator when the heart line is completely flat, but in a real hospital a shock doesn’t help a flat line. TV doctors also do everything themselves: they take the blood, run the scans, operate and talk to the family, all in one shift.

                Still, not everything about TV doctors is bad. Many nurses and doctors say that a series was the first thing that made them interested in medicine, and some programmes ask real doctors to check their scripts. A nurse I know told me that she didn’t mind the mistakes. She said that the best thing a TV doctor could do was make people curious, and then send them to a real one.
