                  Science & the movies

                  Fact or fiction? Five ideas from the screen

                  Science fiction has always been full of things that seem impossible. But how far-fetched are they really?

                  Invisibility. Harry Potter’s cloak is pure fantasy, isn’t it? Not completely. Scientists have made “metamaterials” that bend light around very small objects, so that they seem to disappear. Hiding a whole person in this way faces extreme obstacles, but invisibility is not totally implausible.

                  Teleportation. Physicists can already “teleport” quantum information from one particle to another, over more than a thousand kilometres. Sending a human being is a different story: most scientists think it is far-fetched, to say the least.

                  Time travel. Travelling into the future is, in a tiny way, already real: astronauts on the International Space Station come home a fraction of a second younger than if they had stayed on Earth. Travelling back in time, however, is only speculative.

                  Life on Mars. Space agencies say that a human trip to Mars might be achievable within twenty years. Living there for good is quite a way off: there is no air to breathe and far too much radiation.

                  Reading minds. Brain implants already let some paralysed people move a cursor or type just by thinking. A machine that can read all our thoughts is still a long way from a real possibility, but simple “mind reading” is perfectly plausible.

                    Grammar

                    All, every, both, neither

                    Small words that say how much or how many. Read the rules, then choose. Then make the sentences true.

                    0 / 0

                    all, every, most

                    • all + plural or uncountable noun: All scientists make mistakes.
                    • every + singular noun: Every scientist makes mistakes. Every day.
                    • Not all alone as a subject: use everything / everybody. Everything is ready.
                    • most / all / none of + the, my, them: Most of my friends, but Most people.

                    no, none, any · both, neither, either

                    • no + noun, with a positive verb: There’s no milk. none alone or with of: None of them came.
                    • any = it doesn’t matter which: Call me any time.
                    • both (… and), neither (… nor), either (… or): Neither the food nor the music was good.

                      Make it true
