          The biggest, the most beautiful

          Two things: a comparative. Three or more: a superlative, always with the. Listen to the examples and repeat them.

          • Two things-er / more + thanMoscow is bigger than Kazan.
          • Three or morethe -est / the mostMoscow is the biggest city in Russia.
          • A phrase to learnthe best … I’ve ever …It’s the best pizza I’ve ever eaten!

          How to make it

          AdjectiveRuleSuperlative
          cheap, old, smallthe + estthe cheapest, the oldest
          nice, safethe + stthe nicest, the safest
          big, hotone vowel + one consonant: double itthe biggest, the hottest
          busy, noisy, prettyy → iestthe busiest, the noisiest
          beautiful, crowded, expensivethe most + adjectivethe most beautiful, the most crowded
          good, bad, fardifferent wordthe best, the worst, the furthest

          Listen and repeat

          • Moscow is the biggest city in Russia.
          • This is the oldest building in the town.
          • Saturday is the busiest day at the market.
          • It’s the most beautiful place I’ve ever been to.
          • That was the best pizza I’ve ever eaten!

            Kate’s travel notes

            Three cities, one winner

            This year Kate visited three cities: Rome, Prague and Stockholm. Which one was the best?

            Rome was the hottest and the most crowded. There were tourists everywhere, but the food was fantastic. “It was the best pizza I’ve ever eaten!” says Kate.

            Prague was the cheapest. Kate loved the old town, with its famous bridge and the castle on the hill. It’s the most romantic city of the three.

            Stockholm was the most expensive, but it was also the cleanest and the quietest. Every evening Kate walked by the harbour and watched the boats.

            “For food, Rome. For a cheap weekend, Prague. But for a relaxing weekend, Stockholm is the best,” she says.
