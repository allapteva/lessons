        Online or in the shop?

        A coat for Bella

        Kate has a small white dog called Bella. It’s October, the days are colder, and Bella needs a warm coat.

        In the pet shop near Kate’s flat, a red coat costs £45. It’s beautiful, but it isn’t cheap. Online, Kate found a grey coat for £29.99, with free delivery. It was much cheaper than the red one, and it looked warmer, so she bought it.

        The coat arrived a week later, but it was the wrong size. Bella is bigger than the dog in the photo! Kate sent the coat back and went to the pet shop. She paid more, but she got the red coat on the same day, and the shop assistant helped her find the right size.

        “Online shopping is cheap, but it isn’t as easy as it looks,” says Kate. “Next time I’m going to try things on Bella in the shop!”

          Comparing: the rules

          Two things are different, or two things are the same. Listen to the examples and repeat them.

          • Different-er / more + thanMy car is bigger than yours.
          • The sameas … asMy bag is as big as yours.
          • Lessnot as … asYour car isn’t as big as mine.

          Adjectives

          AdjectiveRuleComparative
          cheap, warm, fast+ ercheaper, warmer, faster
          nice, large+ rnicer, larger
          big, hot, thinone vowel + one consonant: double itbigger, hotter, thinner
          easy, happy, busyy → iereasier, happier, busier
          expensive, beautifulmore + adjectivemore expensive, more beautiful
          good, bad, fardifferent wordbetter, worse, further

          Adverbs: how you do it

          • fast → faster, hard → harder
          • well → better, badly → worse
          • slowly → more slowly, carefully → more carefully. Not slowlier.

          Listen and repeat

          • Lu is smaller than my friend’s dog.
          • My new car is much bigger than my old one.
          • She drives more carefully than me.
          • This coat isn’t as warm as my old one.

            The sound /ə/

            The most common sound in English: short, quiet and lazy. You hear it in -er, in than and in as. Listen and repeat.

            • cheaper /ˈtʃiːpə/
            • better /ˈbetə/
            • than /ðən/
            • as /əz/
            • My car is older than yours.
            • Lu is smaller than your dog.
            • She drives faster than me.
            • This coat isn’t as warm as my old one.

                In a shop

                Listen to each line and say it. Then play the scenes.

                1. AssistantCan I help you?
                2. YouYes, have you got this jacket in a bigger size?
                3. YouCan I try it on? Where’s the fitting room?
                4. YouIt’s nice, but it isn’t as warm as my old one.
                5. YouHave you got anything cheaper?
                6. AssistantThis one is £49.99. It’s 30% off today.
                7. YouGreat. Can I pay by card?
                8. YouCan I have a receipt, please?

                Scene 1: a jacket in London

                You are on holiday in London. It’s colder than you thought, and you need a jacket. Your teacher is the shop assistant. Ask about the size, the price and the fitting room, and pay.

                Scene 2: a coat for Lu

                You are in a pet shop. Lu needs a winter coat. Compare two coats: which is warmer, softer, cheaper, nicer? Then choose one.
