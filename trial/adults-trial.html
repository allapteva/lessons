        Stop trying to be perfect

        Two years ago, my company opened an office in Madrid, and I suddenly needed Spanish. At first I was embarrassed. I could read emails without much trouble, but when people spoke to me, I froze.

        Then a colleague gave me some advice that changed everything. “Stop trying to be perfect,” she said. “Talk to people, make mistakes and laugh about them.” So I did. I started having lunch with the Spanish team every Wednesday, even though I understood only half of the conversation. I watched cartoons with Spanish subtitles and kept a notebook of useful phrases.

        Today I’m still not fluent, but I can explain my ideas in meetings, and nobody expects me to be perfect. Not even me.
