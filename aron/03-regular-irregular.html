    EVENT 2 · TRAINING CAVE

    Why do we need -ed?

    In English, the verb tells you when.

    The past, step by step ✔ was / were→ rule-followers: + ed→ rule-breakers→ next time: didn't / Did …?

    🔁 Every day

    I play Brawl Stars.
    Dan plays chess.

    It happens again and again.

    ⏪ Yesterday

    I played Brawl Stars.
    Dan played chess.

    It happened. It's finished, like a match replay.

    1 · -ed means "finished"

    I walk my dogs. = every day
    I walked my dogs. = yesterday

    No -ed? Then it sounds like every day.

    2 · The same for everyone

    I / you / he / she / we / they played

    No -s in the past: Dan plays → Dan played. Easier than was / were!

    3 · Two teams of verbs

    Rule-followers: play → played
    Rule-breakers: go → went

    Rule-breakers have their own past. You learn them like new words. was and were are rule-breakers too!

    • Every day I walk my dogs. Yesterday I walked my dogs.
    • Rosie usually trains at six. Yesterday she trained at seven.
    • We often play chess. Last Sunday we played chess with Grandpa.
    • Yesterday I was at home and I watched a film.

    Listen: usually or in the past?

    Press the speaker. Listen for the end of the verb. Then choose.

    won sounds just like one. saw rhymes with four. ate rhymes with eight.

    The best match ever

    by Dan Wilson

    Yesterday was a great day! In the morning I went to the pool with Rosie. She swam for an hour, but I only swam for ten minutes. Then we had pancakes at a café.

    In the afternoon I played Brawl Stars with my friends Max and Leo. The first match was terrible. We lost! So we started again. I played Colt, and I shot the red team's Bull three times. Max played Dynamike and threw a bomb at their Shelly. Leo collected ten gems, and we waited for the countdown. We won! We jumped and shouted, and Mum laughed at us.

    In the evening I was tired. I ate two burgers and watched a video about a new brawler. What a day!
